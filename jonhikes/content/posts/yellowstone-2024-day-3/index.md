We woke up with the sun again and lay in our tent for a few minutes. It was a quiet wind free night and we had slept well.

We had 13 miles ahead of us and we didn’t want to wait around, so we grabbed breakfast and headed out. We meandered through a section with lots of cut down trees and lots of young growth. We met a CDT section hiker and chatted for a short bit. We were telling anyone we could about the muck we had gone through the day before.

{{ post_img(path="images/1.HEIC", alt="") }}

After several miles, the trail turned into a wide run down paved road. We walked side by side and chatted away. We met a couple on a day hike up to Shoshone Lake who were very curious about how we had secured our backpacking permit. I told them about the lottery system and how it all worked. Maybe they’ll do some backpacking next year.

Not long after we came to a parking lot. We took a break and dropped all of our trash into the garbage cans there. Not a ton of weight, but it was nice not to have the trash with us anymore. We finished up our break, crossed the road, and continued our hike.

Picking up the trail on the other side of the road, we noticed a lot of hoof prints. We’re not well versed in hoof track identification, so we were not sure what had made the tracks. Several miles in we had our answer: llamas. There were some backpackers with llamas. There’s an idea for a future trip.

{{ post_img(path="images/2.HEIC", alt="") }}

We soon reached the Heart Lake Geyser Basin and started seeing some steamy pools of water. One steaming pool was close enough to the trail that we could feel the heat of the steam.

{{ post_img(path="images/3.HEIC", alt="") }}
{{ post_img(path="images/4.HEIC", alt="") }}
{{ post_img(path="images/5.HEIC", alt="") }}

We were in an open area and the sun was beating down, so we were quite warm even though we started going downhill. We decided to stop next to a creek and quickly learned that it was fed by hot geyser water. Not too hot to burn us, but too hot for drinking and soaking. We took a break before continuing on.

{{ post_img(path="images/6.HEIC", alt="") }}

We reached Heart Lake and, not long afterward, our campsite. It was right by the lake and it had an open air toilet. Open air toilets are preferred over outhouses because there isn’t a structure filled with stink. We hung our food, set up the tent, and then headed down to the lake. There was a hint of algae near the edges, but we didn’t let that keep us from a good dip. Logan even found some fishing lures.

{{ post_img(path="images/7.HEIC", alt="") }}

As dinnertime rolled around, we cooked up creamy garlic spirals and added in bacon bits. It was a little on the watery side, but still delicious. We had brought two new-to-us desserts to try and were going to eat them on our longest days. Today was our second longest so we had brownie bites. They looked like dog kibble, but they tasted like brownies so that was a win. We’ll probably take those again. We finished off dinner with Oreos (of course) and washed things up.

The sun was setting behind the trees today, so we didn’t stay up to watch it go down. We got into the tent, set up our sleeping pads and bags, and chatted a bit before falling asleep. We were both tired and sore from our big mileage day.
