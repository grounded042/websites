The sun woke us up as it started shining through the trees. We got up and slowly made breakfast and picked up camp. It was a little chilly, but some warm oatmeal fixed that for me.

As I ate my oatmeal I turned on the GPS and received a text from my wife: she was pregnant! What an exciting bit of news. The marvels of technology that I can be out in the wilderness and still communicate with her. Logan was excited that he was the second person to learn the news.

We set off down the 10 miles of trail we had for the day. Near the start we came across another hiker. We chatted a bit and he asked us if we were going through the muck. Sure, we naively said. We had no clue what he was talking about.

We wandered through a geyser basin that was steaming and bubbling and erupting with all sorts of thermal features. It seemed like everywhere you turned was something else. We walked along with the smell of rotten eggs wafting through the air. Logan was fascinated by all the moss and made me take pictures.

After finishing the geyser walk we were faced with the muck. And muck it was. A boggy slog of grass and mud with some clear parts here and there with fish in them. We could see the trail signs continuing across the muck and the only way to them was through the muck. We did what any hiker would do: put our crocs on and engaged sport mode. We made our way across and finally came to a small bridge. With clear water running under it we decided to rinse our feet off. We quickly learned that this water was fed from something hot as it felt about the temperature of a hot tub.

On the other side of the muck we switched out of our crocs and headed up the trail. It was the perfect temperature out and we had a smooth trail. A couple of steep parts here and there, but nothing crazy. We stopped for lunch at another campsite and then for water not too long after.

We slowly descended to the lake again and met a CDT hiker as we were about to cross the outlet. We chatted for a bit and wished them well on their journey. Crossing the outlet we hiked a little farther and met two more CDT hikers. We were excited to see other people as we had seen very few compared to our Glacier trip.

Shortly after meeting the hikers we arrived at our campsite. We did the usual: hang food, set up tent, soak in the lake. For dinner we had an amazing cheddar potato soup with bacon bits. The bacon bit bag said 10 servings, but that must have been incorrect as Logan and I split it.

After dinner we hung out by the lake while we waited for the sun to set. We enjoyed the laziness of it and relaxed by tossing rocks in the lake while playing games like trying to hit waves at their peak.

{{ post_img(path="images/11.HEIC", alt="") }}

After the sun went down we turned in for the night. No one else had joined us at our campsite and it was a nice quiet evening.

Food Journal:
- Breakfast: two stroopwafels, peanut butter chocolate chip oatmeal, chocolate covered coffee beans.
- Lunch: protein bars and fruit strips. I didn’t care for them. I like my trail mix better. Really wish I had Canada Dry drink mix.
- Dinner: cheddar potato soup with bacon and tuna. Delicious. Might be one of the best trail meals. Would eat again.
